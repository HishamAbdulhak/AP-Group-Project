UNIVERSITY CREDIT TRANSFER AND PROGRAM CHANGE MANUAL ACADEMIC YEAR 2025-2026
SECTION 1: GUIDELINES FOR INCOMING TRANSFER STUDENTS The university welcomes applications from students currently enrolled in other accredited institutions of higher education who wish to complete their degree at our campus. A transfer applicant is defined as any student who has completed at least 12 credit hours of university-level coursework after graduating from high school. The admission standards for transfer students are rigorous, requiring a minimum cumulative Grade Point Average (GPA) of 3.0 on a 4.0 scale in their previous studies.
The university recognizes that students transfer for a variety of reasons, including relocation, changes in academic interest, or the pursuit of specific programs not offered at their current institution. The primary goal of the transfer policy is to facilitate a smooth transition while ensuring that the academic integrity of the university's degrees is maintained. Students currently under academic suspension or disciplinary probation at their home institution are not eligible to apply until their standing has been resolved.
SECTION 2: THE TRANSFER APPLICATION PROCESS To initiate the process, prospective transfer students must submit a specialized application form separate from the standard first-year entry forms. This ensures that the admissions committee has the specific information required to evaluate prior learning.
The steps to apply for transfer admission are as follows:
1. Complete the Online Transfer Application via the university admissions portal.
2. Pay the non-refundable processing fee.
3. Submit official transcripts from all previously attended colleges and universities. Failure to disclose enrollment at any institution is grounds for immediate denial.
4. Submit a Course Description Handbook or Syllabus for every course for which credit transfer is sought. This allows the faculty to determine the equivalency of the content.
5. Provide a Transfer Report Form signed by the Dean of Students at the current institution, certifying good behavioral standing.
The deadline to apply for the Fall semester is March 1st, and the deadline for the Spring semester is October 15th. Rolling admission is not available for transfer candidates due to the complexity of the credit evaluation process.
SECTION 3: CREDIT EVALUATION AND EQUIVALENCY One of the most critical concerns for transfer students is the determination of how many previous credits will count toward their new degree. The university employs a faculty-led review process to determine credit equivalency.
The following criteria apply to the evaluation of transfer credits:
* Only courses with a grade of C (2.0) or higher are eligible for transfer. Grades of C-, D, or Pass/Fail are generally not accepted.
* The course content must overlap with the university's own curriculum by at least 75 percent.
* A maximum of 60 credit hours may be transferred from a two-year community college.
* A maximum of 90 credit hours may be transferred from a four-year university.
* Regardless of the number of credits transferred, the student must complete their final 30 credit hours in residence at the university to be awarded a degree.
Once the evaluation is complete, the student receives a Preliminary Credit Audit. This document outlines exactly which previous courses have been accepted and which requirements they satisfy in the new degree plan.
SECTION 4: INTERNAL PROGRAM TRANSFERS Current students who wish to change their major or move between faculties within the university must follow the Internal Transfer procedure. This is not automatic; high-demand programs such as Engineering and Nursing often have limited seats and higher GPA requirements for internal transfers.
To apply for an internal change of major:
* The student must have completed at least one semester in their current program.
* The student must meet with an Academic Advisor from the target department to discuss eligibility.
* An Internal Change of Curriculum form must be submitted to the Registrar's Office before the registration period for the upcoming term begins.
If the application is approved, the student's degree audit will be updated. It is important to note that changing majors may result in a delay in graduation, as some previously earned credits may only count as free electives in the new program structure.
